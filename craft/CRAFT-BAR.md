# Craft bar — the premium standard every video is held to

The single source of truth for "good." Producers **aim at this**; reviewers **judge against it and drive the work up to it** — a weak piece is not passed, it is sent back with the specific change that raises it. Distilled from teardowns of real, high-reach software launch videos and a professional anti-AI-slop checklist. Brand/product names are deliberately absent — these are principles, not imitations.

The test, for producer and reviewer alike: **would the company whose product this is actually ship this as their launch video?** Not "is it acceptable," not "does the JSON look plausible." If the honest answer is no, it is not done.

---

## 1. Structure & content

- **One arc, no feature dump.** hook → (problem, only if the audience is cold / the category is unknown) → product reveal → **2–4 hero features, not a list** → proof → CTA. Feature-dumping is the single most common failure. More than 4 "here's another thing" beats = revise.
- **The hook earns the rest in the first 1–5s, and is NEVER a logo/brand card.** The strongest openers, in order: (a) open **in-product on one real high-stakes action** (type the real question, trigger the real thing); (b) a **benefit/desire question** over mood, product name withheld a few seconds; (c) the **recognizable artifact materializing** (the UI assembles from nothing). A cold open on a logo, a brand name, or a generic title card is the measurably weakest option — forbid it.
- **On-screen CTA is REQUIRED and scored.** End on an explicit in-frame ask: a labeled action (e.g. a "Start free" pill), or a concrete next step, held readable to the end. A bare end-URL or a soft tagline with no ask is a fail — this is the one beat most videos get lazy on, and it is exactly the beat that converts.
- **Prove with capability, legibly.** Show the artifact doing the thing, with the one strongest specific number/receipt **large enough to read** (its own card if it's the hero number). Vague "it's powerful" with nothing on screen = revise. Social proof (logos/metrics) is optional and never a substitute for capability-proof.
- **Length keys off aspect/placement, not one number.** 9:16 social ≤ ~15s · teaser 15–40s · 16:9 hero 30–90s. Do not pad to hit a number.
- **Script density is a ceiling: ≤ ~2.5 words/sec.** Airy (≈1.7 wps) is correct for a cinematic register; never pad a cinematic piece to a word count.

## 2. Visual craft — the frame (this is where weak work actually fails)

- **One dominant subject per beat, filling ≥ ~50% of the frame.** The thing the VO names at that moment must be the clear, readable anchor. A big empty ring/band/gradient with the real content tiny in a corner = dead space = revise. Scale the focal element up (raise `zoom`, aim `focus` at it).
- **Clear focal hierarchy.** One hero element; everything else is subordinate and smaller. When several similar elements share a beat, the active one is visibly larger or ringed while the rest recede — a tiny caption is not enough.
- **Every beat is visually distinct. No slideshow.** Near-duplicate held cards (the same card across 3–4 beats with a slow zoom, a line repeated over the same frame) read as padding — cut them or change the frame. Dead static holds, especially a frozen sparse end card, are the worst offenders.
- **Motion must mean something.** The merge animates, the chart draws, the number counts up, the camera moves over a canvas to reveal. A slow zoom on a static card is not motion. Zero real cuts/transitions on the VO beats = slideshow = revise.
- **Give the hero number its own full-frame moment.** The single strongest metric/receipt earns its own card, not a footnote.
- **One signature move.** Every memorable launch video has one device that is unmistakably *this* product's (a recurring motif, a tempo break, a bespoke transition). A video with no signature is forgettable, however clean.

## 3. Look — must not read as AI slop (the cluster test)

A design should look like it could **only** belong to this project. Detection is by **cluster**, not any single choice: 0–1 tells reads as deliberate; **2–3 reads as lightly-edited AI output; 4+ reads as unedited AI output → revise.** The common tells:

- **Gradients everywhere** (the biggest tell), **a card/rounded-rect for everything**, a single bright accent on a dark/near-black ground, hairline rules, emoji as headings.
- **Unspecified-default fonts** — if the type wasn't chosen *for this brand*, it's a tell. Treat as defaults: Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Arial, Helvetica, Montserrat, Poppins, system-ui (tier 1); Space Grotesk, Geist, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta, Manrope, **Outfit**, Syne, Instrument Sans/Serif, Fraunces, Playfair (tier 2 — now just as much a tell). Swapping one default for another does not fix it.
- **Every look choice must trace to the brand's own material** (its real product, palette, era, audience, content). "Clean / modern / lets it breathe" is not a reason. **Swap test:** put a different product's name on it — if it still fits, it's generic, revise.
- **No house style.** Do not reuse a signature or palette from a previous video. Each starts from the brand's material, not from the last good design.

Aim for **0–1 tells**. Count them explicitly; 2+ is a revise with the specific swaps named (and the swap must be *sourced*, not another default).

## 4. Sound (when the render has audio)

- VO is not enough: a low music bed that supports and a few deliberate SFX on key beats. Bare first-generation TTS with flat dynamics and true silence in every gap is an immediate AI tell.
- No dead tail: the piece ends on its last meaningful beat, not several seconds of static hold.

---

## How reviewers use this

1. Judge against the ship test above, at the level of a top studio — not "acceptable."
2. Walk §1–§4. For **every** failure, name the specific beat/element, say what's wrong, and give the **concrete change that raises it** (the number to change, the element to scale, the default to swap and to what-sourced-choice). The job is to bump the work up, not just to grade it.
3. Count the §3 tell cluster explicitly. 2+ → revise.
4. "No weak board/cut passes." With the redo budget set high, keep sending it back until it clears the bar. Passing mediocrity is the failure mode this bar exists to prevent.
