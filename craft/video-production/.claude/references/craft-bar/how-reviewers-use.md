## How reviewers use this

1. Judge against the **ship test** above, at the level of a top studio — not "acceptable."
2. **Walk §A on every video** (frame craft, proof-legibly, the §3 tell cluster, legibility/pacing, sound). Then apply the **matching §B profile** for structure/arc/close/length. Judge the work against *both*. For **every** failure, name the specific beat/element, say what's wrong, and give the **concrete change that raises it** (the number to change, the element to scale, the default font to swap and to what-sourced-choice, the beat to cut). The job is to bump the work up, not just to grade it.
3. **Count the §A3 tell cluster explicitly. 2+ → revise** with sourced swaps named.
4. **Score the craft sub-axes separately — never blend them.** Report five axes, not two lumped ones: **Structure** (the beat arc — right beats, right order, no padding) · **Temporal** (timing, easing, transition smoothness, cadence, and §A4 dwell — how the motion *reads over time*) · **Composition** (balance, contrast, typography, hierarchy — §A1 framing) · **mechanical Craft** (clipping, alignment, clean render — the render executed cleanly) · **Look** (§A3 anti-slop). A failure on one must not be diluted by a passing neighbour it used to share a score with.
5. **Weight Temporal and mechanical Craft the hardest.** These are the failure-prone axes a model judge grades worst (Code2Video), while brief-conformance is near-solved — so justify Temporal and mechanical Craft with specific timecodes and let the conformance/coverage checks stay a cheap checklist. Where the deterministic layout pre-check (issue 0033) already decided a mechanical case from the render's own geometry (clipping, never-settling, low contrast, >1 type system), it has reopened — don't re-litigate it; spend judgement on what geometry can't capture.
6. **"No weak board/cut passes."** With the redo budget set high, keep sending it back until it clears the bar. Passing mediocrity is the failure mode this bar exists to prevent.
