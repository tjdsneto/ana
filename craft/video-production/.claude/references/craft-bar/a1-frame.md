### A1. Frame craft — this is where weak work actually fails
- **One dominant subject per beat, filling ≥ ~50% of the frame.** The thing the VO names at that moment must be the clear, readable anchor. A big empty ring/band/gradient with the real content tiny in a corner = dead space = revise. Scale the focal element up (raise `zoom`, aim `focus` at it).
- **Clear focal hierarchy.** One hero element; everything else is subordinate and smaller. When several similar elements share a beat, the active one is visibly larger or ringed while the rest recede — a tiny caption is not enough.
- **Breathing room — do not overload the frame.** One idea per beat, with margin around it; elements have space to breathe inside the ≥96px safe area. Cramming competing blocks, text run to the edges, or more than the eye takes in at once = revise. (Breathing room is not the same as dead space: dead space is a *small* subject adrift in a *large* empty frame — fix that by scaling the subject up, not by filling the void with clutter.)
- **Path of focus — guide the eye to the one thing that matters.** Every beat has a single clear entry point and a deliberate route: the hero element pulls the eye first (through scale, contrast, motion, or position on a strong line), supporting elements second. The information the viewer must read sits where attention lands, not in a low-attention corner, and something (motion, a ring, a cursor, scale) actively draws the eye to it. Alignment and spacing are consistent, not ad hoc. If nothing directs the eye to the main thing, revise.
- **Every beat is visually distinct. No slideshow.** Near-duplicate held cards (the same card across 3–4 beats with a slow zoom, a line repeated over the same frame) read as padding — cut them or change the frame. Dead static holds, especially a frozen sparse end card, are the worst offenders.
- **Motion is the default, not a decoration.** A finished video looks *alive* — things reveal, move, respond, transition — never a flat slideshow of held cards. This is the baseline for essentially every video, not a flourish reserved for some. **Motion must mean something:** the merge animates, the chart draws, the number counts up, the camera moves over a canvas to reveal, a cursor acts. A slow zoom on a static card is not motion. Zero real cuts/transitions on the VO beats = slideshow = revise. (The renderable vocabulary — the verbs and transitions available — is in MOTION-VOCAB.md; every beat declares one.)
- **Give the single strongest number/receipt its own full-frame moment.** When a video has one hero metric, it earns its own card, not a footnote. (Not every type has such a number — see §B; but when one exists, frame it big.)
- **One signature move.** Every *memorable* video has one device that is unmistakably *this* product's (a recurring motif, a tempo break, a bespoke transition). A video with no signature is forgettable, however clean. This is a taste requirement, not a launch-video requirement.
