### A4. Legibility & pacing (type-independent)
- **The hook earns the rest in the first 1–5s, and is NEVER a logo/brand card.** The strongest openers, in order: (a) open **in-product on one real high-stakes action** (type the real question, trigger the real thing); (b) a **benefit/desire question** over mood, product name withheld a few seconds; (c) the **recognizable artifact materializing** (the UI assembles from nothing). A cold open on a logo, a brand name, or a generic title card is the measurably weakest option — forbid it, in every type.
- **One idea per beat.** Don't stack two messages on one beat; if the VO says two things, it's two beats.
- **Length keys off aspect/placement, not one number.** 9:16 social ≤ ~15s · teaser 15–40s · 16:9 hero 30–90s. Do not pad to hit a number. (The *target within the band* is the type's call — §B.)
- **Script density is a ceiling: ≤ ~2.5 words/sec.** Airy (≈1.7 wps) is correct for a cinematic register; never pad a cinematic piece to a word count.
- **Dwell — every information beat holds long enough to be read.** Pacing is *read-time*, not clock-time. After a beat's text / number / diagram finishes revealing, it stays fully composed and still long enough to read at a comfortable pace before the next beat or the transition starts: roughly **≥1.5–2.0s of settle** on a single line or number, **2.5–3s** on a beat carrying multiple rows or figures. A beat whose content lands and immediately cuts is a rush = revise. **Do not buy time back by speeding the reveals or shrinking the holds** — if the runtime is tight, drop a lesser beat or merge two, never rush the key ones; let the piece run a few seconds longer instead. Trim only *genuine dead air* (flat holds at joins where nothing new is being read), never the time a viewer needs to comprehend.
- **Reserve caption room when burned-in captions are called for.** When the brief or the §B type asks for burned subtitles (silent-autoplay / social cuts), keep a caption-safe band clear of the focal element so the burned text never collides with or crops the subject. This is conditional — only when captions are specified, not on every video.
- **End on the last meaningful beat.** Whether that beat is a hard CTA, a deferred tease, or a resolve is type-dependent (§B) — but it is never several seconds of dead static hold.
