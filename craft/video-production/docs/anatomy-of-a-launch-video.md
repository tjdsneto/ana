# Anatomy of a software launch video

How a short product/feature launch video is built, as general craft. Tool-agnostic. Use it to judge whether a script, storyboard, or finished cut holds the bar.

## The arc

Most effective launch videos follow one spine:

**hook → (problem) → product reveal → 2–4 features → proof → call to action.**

- **Hook (first 2–5s).** Earn the rest. Open on a recognizable artifact or a desire, **not** a logo card. Strongest openers: the product mid–real-action (type the real question, cut just before the result), or a benefit/question over mood with the name held a few seconds. A logo cold-open is the measurably weaker choice.
- **Problem (conditional).** Name the pain in the viewer's words. **Skip it for a warm audience or a known category** — only cold/unknown-category products need the explicit problem beat.
- **Reveal.** Introduce the product as the answer to the hook.
- **Features — pick 2–4, not a dump.** Feature-dumping is the most common failure. Show a few, each as a concrete moment, not a list.
- **Proof.** Two kinds, and they are not equal:
  - **Capability proof** — show the artifact doing the thing (a real result, a real number, the actual output). Specific, defensible, strong. Prefer it.
  - **Social proof** — logos, metrics, testimonials. Useful for cold B2B; often front-loaded. Weaker on its own than capability proof.
- **Call to action (CTA).** An explicit on-screen next step ("Start free", a concrete action), held readable to the end — not a bare URL or a soft tagline. This is the single most commonly fumbled beat; an in-frame CTA is a real advantage.

## Length by placement (not one number)

Target length follows **where it plays and its shape**, not a universal rule of thumb:

- **9:16 social** — short, ~≤15s.
- **Teaser** — 15–40s.
- **16:9 hero / full demo** — 30–90s.

Pick the aspect ratio for the platform first; length follows.

## Show the real thing (B2B)

Buyers discount claims they can't see. Show at least one real workflow in the actual interface. Stylized/illustrative UI is the exception — justified only for confidential or over-dense screens — and when used it should read as **clearly stylized**, not a fake of the real product. How to put that app on screen well — device framing, directing the eye, interaction as motion, UI sound — is its own craft: see [showing-a-web-app.md](showing-a-web-app.md).

## Pacing

- Cut to the voice: land beats and reveals on spoken words, not arbitrary seconds.
- No near-duplicate beats — if two shots look nearly the same, cut one; repetition to fill time reads as a slideshow.
- Narration density is a **ceiling, not a target**: dense explainers run ~2–2.5 words/sec; a cinematic piece can be far airier. Don't pad a mood film to hit a word count.
- One idea per beat; one dominant subject per frame (it should fill the frame, not sit tiny amid dead space).

## Signature move

The memorable launch videos each have one recognizable device — a morphing hook, a version-stamp motif, a drumroll end-card, a presenter who shrinks to a corner over the product. Pick one and make it the thing people remember; don't stack five.

## Quick checklist

- Hook in the first seconds, not a logo card.
- Problem beat only if the audience is cold.
- 2–4 features, each a concrete moment.
- At least one capability-proof beat (a real result/number).
- An explicit, readable on-screen CTA.
- Aspect ratio chosen for the platform; length to match.
- Real UI shown at least once.
- One idea per beat; subject fills the frame; beats distinct; cuts on the voice.
